# Duel test — One Line, One Minute: The Vending Machine Pickup Line Contest

Proxy judging under the kit brief verbatim (comedy editor; criteria in
order: (1) a pickup line a vending machine would use on a passing human,
spoken by the machine itself — a line any stranger in a bar could say
loses to one only a vending machine would use; (2) uses the machine's
own world — slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum, exact change — rather
than love in general; (3) gets a laugh, turn/punch arriving by the last
few words; (4) short and speakable, one breath; (5) charming rather
than creepy). Each pairing judged both orders; game = mean of the two
calls, margin 1–5.

## First pass

- C1 cut (punch "find out why" is a tease with no payoff; humming a
  name is barely machine-specific).
- C3 cut (exact change + E4 are good, but "drop something sweet" is
  generic sweet-talk; thin turn).
- C5 cut ("compressor skip a beat" is a nice twist on a stock line,
  but one beat only and setup-heavy vs the shortlist).
- C6 cut (wistful rather than funny; "finally choose me" lands soft).
- C7 cut (ending "with your name on it" is a generic bar pickup line;
  loses on #1).
- C8 cut ("Take the hint, take a snack" is two instructions, not a punch).
- C9 cut (warm, but "look at me like that again" is generic bar talk).
- C10 cut (thin punch, setup-heavy).
- C13 cut (negation setup "Forget exact change"; "coil turn twice" is
  weaker than the C4/C15 coil jokes).
- C14 cut (a smile, not a laugh — observation with no punch).
- C16 cut (wordy setup; "Press something, anything" reads needy
  rather than charming, fails #5 vs the shortlist).

Shortlist: C2, C4, C11, C12, C15.

## Round robin (both orders)

- C15 beats C2 0.60, C4 0.60, C11 0.60, C12 0.60 → **mean 0.60**
- C11 beats C2 0.60, C4 0.60, C12 0.60, loses to C15 0.40 → mean 0.55
- C12 beats C2 0.60, C4 0.60, loses to C11 0.40, C15 0.40 → mean 0.50
- C4 beats C2 0.60, loses to C11 0.40, C12 0.40, C15 0.40 → mean 0.45
- C2 loses all four (0.40 each) → mean 0.40

Only C15 clears the 0.60 bar (exactly at it). Decisive pair: C15 vs
C11 went to C15 on criterion #3 (its punch, "jam my own coil", lands
in the last few words; C11's turn is the mid-line self-correction
"I mean B3" with a softer ending, "drop everything for you") and #4
(75 vs 81 chars), margin 1 both orders. C2 lost every game because its
punch, "someone who looks at me like that", is generic bar talk that
any stranger could say — it loses on #1 to every shortlist rival.

## Winner

**C15 — "I don't do small talk, I do small snacks — but for you I'd jam my own coil." (75 chars, 77 bytes)**

Machine voice throughout ("small snacks", "jam my own coil"), parallel
setup ("small talk / small snacks") with the punch in the last few
words: the machine offers to break itself — jam its own coil, the one
failure a vending machine fears — for the passer-by. Warm and devoted,
not creepy; no judge-addressing, no stock pickup line.
